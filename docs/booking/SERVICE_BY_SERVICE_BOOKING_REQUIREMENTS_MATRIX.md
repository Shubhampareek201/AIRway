## Common customer booking shell (proposed)
1. Select service and configure only the inputs relevant to that service.
2. Show package/variant, quantity and add-ons when configured; update total and duration immediately.
3. Collect service-specific details and optional photos/notes; validate required answers before continuing.
4. Select/confirm the service address first, then load date/time availability for that address and service; never show a slot unless the backend confirms it.
5. Show a complete review: service, variant, quantity, extras, answers, address, slot, duration and price/quote status.
6. Create booking only after server-side revalidation of catalogue, price snapshot, city eligibility and slot availability.

## Category journey patterns (draft)
- **Beauty:** Select service → choose package/treatment where applicable → answer only relevant service questions → optionally choose professional preference (Female professional / No preference) → confirm address → select an API-confirmed date/time → review.
- **Car Wash:** Choose vehicle type → choose wash/detailing service → select package/vehicle variant → vehicle make/model and access details → date & time → address → review.
- **Electrical:** Choose appliance/issue and work type → quantity/property/material → describe issue + optional photos → show fixed price or inspection/visit-fee explanation → date & time → address → review.
- **Home Cleaning:** Choose room/area/property → choose package/depth → enter size/count where needed → select add-ons → optional photos/access notes → date & time → address → review.

## Electrical — detailed professional-service requirements (design review)

### Design standard: configure each job like a qualified electrician

The customer should not need to diagnose an electrical fault or understand technical jargon. Ask what they observe, what they want installed/repaired, where it is, and whether there are visible warning signs. The electrician is responsible for technical diagnosis, isolation, testing and deciding whether the work is safe and within their qualification.

Use conditional questions by service. Do not ask every customer for "problem", "work type", "number of items", "property" and "material" regardless of the service. A fan installation needs fan/mounting/ceiling details; a power-failure diagnosis needs the affected area and symptoms; a new-wiring job needs a site assessment.

### Safety-first entry and emergency handling

- At the start of any fault-related journey, show a short warning-sign check: active sparks/arcing, smoke/fire, burning smell, hot/melting switchboard, electric shock/tingling, water touching electrical equipment, or exposed live conductors.
- If any immediate danger is reported, stop the normal booking flow. Tell the customer not to touch the equipment or exposed wires, keep people away, and isolate the main supply only if it can be done safely without approaching the hazard. If there is fire/smoke or risk to life, leave the area and contact local emergency/fire services. Do not tell an unqualified customer to open a panel, test a circuit or repair wiring.
- Route urgent hazards to an urgent qualified response channel only if AIRway actually operates one. Otherwise state clearly that AIRway's normal appointment is not an emergency response and direct the customer to appropriate local emergency/utility assistance.
- Capture safety warnings and urgency for the worker before assignment. Do not allow a worker to accept a job beyond their qualifications or required protective equipment.
- Never promise a fixed repair price before fault diagnosis where the cause, parts or extent are unknown.

### Customer journey order

1. Select the exact electrical item/service or choose "I am not sure" and describe the symptom in plain language.
2. Choose the work intent only where it applies: installation, repair, replacement, inspection or diagnosis. Do not ask this for a service whose name already defines the work.
3. Collect the relevant quantity, location/room, equipment details and observable symptoms. Ask for a photo of the outside/visible area only; never ask customers to remove covers or expose wiring to take a photo.
4. Ask whether the item/material is already available, customer-supplied, or whether they need the electrician to supply it. For electrical safety parts, final compatibility must be verified by the electrician; customer selection is not proof of suitability.
5. Show fixed labour scope/price only where the task and inclusions are genuinely fixed. For inspection/quote, disclose the configured visit fee before booking and explain that additional work requires a written itemised estimate and explicit customer approval. Do not assume fee refund/adjustment rules.
6. Confirm address, property type, access, floor/height/ladder conditions where relevant, power availability and any society/site restrictions. Then load backend-confirmed slots based on the required skill and realistic duration.
7. Review service, item/quantity, symptoms, materials, visit-fee or quote status, safety notes, address, slot and exclusions. Server must revalidate before creating the booking.

### Professional capability coverage — full electrical service taxonomy

Use this as a completeness and worker-skill checklist, not permission to publish all services immediately. Each family requires qualified and verified workers, tools, parts sourcing, safety process, realistic duration and approved pricing.

- **Lighting and fixtures:** bulbs, tube lights, LED panels, downlights, ceiling lights, chandeliers, wall lights, outdoor/flood lights, decorative/festive lighting, strip/cove lighting, drivers and dimmers.
- **Fans and ventilation:** ceiling, wall, exhaust, decorative fans; regulator/capacitor/bearing diagnosis; mounting/support; bathroom/kitchen exhaust and ventilation controls.
- **Switches, sockets and points:** switches, dimmers, modular boards, standard/16A sockets, USB sockets, plug points, additional points, doorbells, burnt/loose terminals and dedicated appliance outlets.
- **Protection and distribution:** fuse, MCB, RCCB/RCD/ELCB, distribution boards, circuit additions, overload/tripping diagnosis, surge protection and safe circuit labelling.
- **Wiring and fault finding:** new wiring, rewiring, concealed/surface conduit, wiring repair, short circuit, power failure, voltage fluctuation, loose neutral/connection, earthing and electrical safety audits.
- **Geyser and fixed appliance connections:** geyser installation/electrical connection/isolator faults, dedicated circuits, exhaust/ventilation electrical work. Plumbing and appliance internal repairs are separate skills unless verified.
- **Inverter, UPS and backup:** installation, wiring/changeover, battery installation/replacement, charging/output diagnosis, UPS setup/repair and backup-load assessment.
- **Motors and pumps:** water-pump electrical connection, motor wiring, starter/control panel diagnosis and repair; larger/industrial systems require specialist qualification.
- **Smart home and low voltage:** smart switches/lights, sensors, doorbells, controllers, Wi-Fi pairing, low-voltage door access/intercom and automation setup. Network/router work may need a separate skill.
- **Commercial/specialist work to assess separately:** three-phase supply, industrial panels, generator/ATS, solar PV/inverter, EV charger, lift/elevator, fire alarm, CCTV/access control and high-voltage work. Never list these as routine domestic electrician jobs without appropriate licensing/qualification, insurance and safety procedures.

### Minimum worker handoff and job lifecycle

- Exact service, intent (install/repair/replacement/inspection) and each item/quantity.
- Property type, room/location, equipment make/model/rating where known; preserve "customer unsure" rather than forcing guesses.
- Symptoms, warning signs, urgency and customer-visible history; optional photos clearly labelled as customer supplied.
- Material responsibility and any customer-supplied part details; worker must verify compatibility and condition before fitting.
- Access constraints: floor, ceiling height, ladder/scaffold requirement, parking/site entry, power availability and whether property is occupied.
- Required worker skill tags, certifications/experience, tools, PPE and second-worker requirement; matching must filter by actual capability.
- For quote jobs: inspection checklist, visit fee disclosure, measured scope, itemised labour/materials/taxes, estimate validity policy (to be defined), customer accept/reject, and explicit approval before any chargeable extra work.
- Worker records diagnosis, work performed, parts used, test results, before/after evidence where appropriate and customer handover. Never mark a safety-critical job complete without relevant functional/safety tests.
- If the issue is outside scope, worker must stop safely, explain why and route to appropriate specialist; no unapproved substitutions or extra charges.

### Negative and exception scenarios to design

- Emergency warning signs entered during booking; normal booking must stop and urgent safety guidance must appear.
- Customer does not know the equipment model/rating; allow "not sure" and let worker assess.
- Customer selects installation but no product/material is available; clearly disclose supply responsibility and avoid booking an impossible job.
- Customer-supplied component is incompatible, damaged or unsafe; worker must not install it and must seek approval for alternatives.
- Multiple items across different rooms/circuits; collect per-item details when scope differs, not one shared answer that hides complexity.
- High ceiling, heavy chandelier, inaccessible DB, locked utility room, no parking/site permit or power unavailable.
- Worker lacks qualification, tools, parts, ladder/scaffold, second person or required skill; do not offer that slot.
- Fault changes after booking, additional wiring/parts required, quote is higher than expected, customer rejects quote or only approves part of it.
- Utility-side outage or sealed meter issue outside AIRway scope; refer appropriately, do not attempt unauthorised work.
- Repeated MCB/RCCB trips, hot/burnt socket, shock sensation, wet electrical area or exposed conductors; stop unsafe work and escalate.
- Slot conflict, worker rejection, reassignment, customer cancellation, no access, incomplete job, failed test or customer disputes completion.
- Do not allow an electrician to mark "resolved" if protective device has been bypassed, an unsafe temporary joint remains, or the cause of repeated tripping is unknown.

### Complete professional cleaning capability map

This is a coverage checklist for evaluating future catalogue breadth. It is not permission to publish every service. Each capability needs trained workers, tools, consumables, safe method, waste handling, realistic duration and approved pricing.

- **Room and property cleaning:** bedrooms, living/dining, kitchen, bathrooms, common areas, stairs, corridors, full-home regular/deep, move-in/move-out, vacant-property turnover and post-renovation cleaning (specialist).
- **Floor and hard surfaces:** tile, grout, marble, granite, vitrified/porcelain, natural stone, concrete, wood/laminate, vinyl, outdoor decking; scrubbing, stain treatment, stone restoration/polishing as distinct skill.
- **Soft furnishings:** sofa, sectional, recliner, dining chairs, office chairs, curtains/blinds, mattresses, carpets, rugs, cushions and upholstery; fibre-specific cleaning and drying.
- **Glass and façade-accessible surfaces:** interior windows, doors, partitions, mirrors, shower glass, balcony glass, frames/tracks/screens; high-rise/exterior access only through a specialist safety programme.
- **Kitchen and appliances:** counters, sinks, cabinets, backsplash, chimney exterior/interior, hob/cooktop, oven, microwave, refrigerator, dishwasher exterior, exhaust fan; appliance disassembly/repair and gas/electrical connections are separate trades.
- **Bathroom and sanitation:** toilets, basins, showers, bathtubs, fittings, tiles, grout, hard-water scale, exhaust fans and accessible drains; plumbing, sewer and biohazard remediation separate.
- **Outdoor areas:** balconies, terraces, patios, outdoor floors, garden cleanup, lawns, hedges, leaves and green waste; tree work, pesticide application and hazardous waste need specialist handling.
- **Specialist hygiene/restoration:** post-construction dust, mould remediation, flood/water damage, fire/soot cleanup, hoarding, pest/biohazard/medical waste, high-level industrial cleaning. These must not be offered as ordinary home cleaning without specialist training, PPE, containment and disposal compliance.
- **Commercial cleaning (future scope):** shops, offices, clinics, schools, common areas, washrooms, periodic contracts and after-hours cleaning. Requires staffing, access/security, insurance and documented service-level standards.

### Minimum worker handoff and operational requirements

- Exact room/item count, package, selected add-ons, size/area estimates and customer-defined priority areas.
- Surface/material and condition, stain/odour notes, photos where useful, and any customer "not sure" response.
- Explicit included/excluded task list; do not rely on package names alone.
- Water, electricity, drainage, ventilation, parking, stairs/lift, floor level and safe access constraints.
- Furniture/appliance movement: what worker may move, what customer must empty/unplug/prepare, and what is excluded due to weight or fragility.
- Pets, children, elderly occupants, allergies/sensitivities and preferred fragrance-free/low-odour products where offered; do not make unsupported hypoallergenic claims.
- Worker skills (routine cleaner, deep cleaner, upholstery extractor, stone specialist, glass-access specialist, gardener), team size, equipment, PPE and estimated duration.
- Consumables/material responsibility and safe chemical handling. Workers must follow label dilution/contact time, never mix incompatible products, label containers and keep chemicals away from children/pets/food.
- Before/after evidence for agreed tasks, customer consent for photos, completion checklist and customer sign-off.
- If actual condition/area exceeds booked scope, pause, explain the difference and obtain explicit approval for a revised quote or additional slot before doing extra paid work.

### Negative and exception scenarios to design

- Customer selects deep clean but condition is heavy construction residue, mould, biohazard, pest infestation or hazardous waste beyond normal capability.
- Area size/count exceeds package boundary; worker capacity/time estimate is insufficient.
- Customer is unsure of surface/material; worker must inspect/test a small inconspicuous area before using chemicals.
- Delicate/antique fabric, natural stone, coated/tinted glass, damaged grout, loose fixtures or pre-existing damage.
- Stain/odour is permanent or cannot be guaranteed removable; communicate realistic outcomes before treatment.
- No water, electricity, drainage, ventilation, parking or safe access; equipment cannot be operated.
- Heavy furniture/appliance needs moving, items are not emptied/unplugged, fragile belongings are not secured, or customer is absent.
- Pet/child access to wet floors or chemicals; cleaning team must secure work zone and communicate drying/re-entry guidance.
- High windows/balconies, unstable ladders, wet/slippery terrace, extreme heat/rain or unsafe outdoor conditions.
- Add-on duplicates a task already included in package; prevent duplicate charge.
- Worker lacks extraction machine, pressure washer, stone-safe tools, ladder/PPE, disposal route or specialist skill.
- Customer requests extra rooms/items mid-job; get price/time approval before proceeding.
- Worker discovers plumbing/electrical/appliance repair requirement; stop and route to relevant service, do not perform unqualified work.
- Worker rejection/no-show, reassignment, customer cancellation, no access, incomplete checklist, equipment failure, weather interruption or disputed quality.
- Never mark job complete if booked areas/tasks were skipped or unsafe chemical residue/wet-floor hazards remain.
